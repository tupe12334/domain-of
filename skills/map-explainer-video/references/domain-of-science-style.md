# The Domain of Science "Map of X" style

Source: a study of four videos by Dominic Walliman (Domain of Science, youtube.com/@DomainofScience): The Map of Mathematics, The Map of Physics, Map of Computer Science and The Map of Chemistry. I read their transcripts and contact sheets of frames taken every 12 seconds. This file describes the style so that new work can follow its *method*. It is not a template for copying his maps.

## Visual language

- **One world map per field.** The whole subject is one continuous illustrated map. Main branches are territories with soft, organic borders, like countries. Each has its own flat colour: pastel lilac, pink, sky blue, mint, tan.
- **The start is in the centre.** In Mathematics, a tan "ORIGINS" disc (counting, the first equations, the first zero) sits in the middle, and the branches grow outwards from it.
- **Big region titles.** Thick-outlined boxes with a flat fill (often yellow, magenta or orange) and bold hand-lettered capitals. They stay readable even in the full-map shot.
- **Small labels with doodles.** Each subtopic has a small all-caps label in a thin-outlined box, and next to it a cartoon icon: a Rubik's cube for group theory, a bell curve for statistics, a Turing machine tape. Small handwritten sub-text adds a formula, a date or an example.
- **Portraits of key people.** Small cartoon heads of the founders (Newton, Einstein, Turing), sometimes in a circle frame, placed next to their ideas.
- **Metaphor landforms.** Physics has a "Chasm of Ignorance" between quantum theory and relativity. Philosophy is a cloud that floats above the map. Mathematics is the bedrock under physics. Foundations and unknowns become features of the land.
- **Dark or flat background** until the regions are revealed. In the opening of Mathematics, a single disc sits on black.
- **Hand-drawn finish.** Thick black outlines, flat fills, slightly wobbly lines, marker-style lettering. Nothing photographic, no gradients.

## Motion

- **The camera tours the map.** It pans and zooms smoothly from area to area as the narration moves.
- **Pop-in on cue.** Items appear the moment their name is spoken, with a quick scale-up. The map fills up while the viewer watches.
- **Regular zoom-outs.** After each section, the camera pulls back to show progress and where the next area sits.
- **The final reveal.** The camera ends on the whole dense map, which works as a poster. It is often shown with a signature in the corner.
- **A short cold open** before the map: a small character doodle or a quick visual hook on a plain background.

## Narration voice

- First person, calm, curious, a little self-deprecating ("okay, enough of this hyperbole", "I should have written energy everywhere on this map").
- One plain sentence defines each term: "Thermodynamics is the description of energy and how it passes from one form to another."
- Concrete examples instead of abstractions: lift on an aeroplane wing, a Rubik's cube as a permutation group, the halting problem.
- Wonder at crossovers: "this kind of thing has happened so many times… which is amazing I think."
- About 150 words per minute. A map video runs 8–11 minutes and covers about 1,400–1,900 words.

## His usual structure (for reference, not a template)

His "Map of X" videos about broad fields mostly follow this order. Learn from it, but do not copy it into every video: a subject with a different shape (a journey, a stack, a tree) needs a different structure. What carries over is the style and the teaching moves, not this sequence.

1. **Hook:** what we learn at school is one corner of a huge field.
2. **Origins:** a fast run through history, in the centre of the map.
3. **The split:** "it can be broadly broken down into N parts", with each territory named in turn.
4. **The tour:** each territory, subtopic by subtopic, one sentence each.
5. **Crossovers:** links between territories.
6. **The edges:** foundations, unknowns and big questions (a chasm, a cloud, bedrock).
7. **Personal reflection:** why the narrator loves the subject.
8. **"So that is the map of X."** The full map is on screen.
